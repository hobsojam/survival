# The Castle Design

## The long view

The game becomes effectively open-ended through a run of huge goals. Each one feels like the final thing and really does get finished, and then there is one more. *(The user: "ideally we have a long list of 'oh look, this is the final thing' but it isnt. there is always one more thing.")*

| Goal | In-game years | Whose figure |
|---|---|---|
| Keep (built) | 2–3 | Measured by the playthrough check |
| Castle (this project) | About 5 | Mine; the user asked for "exponentially longer" and accepted this table's scale |
| Town | About 10 | Mine |
| Palace, with an observatory | About 20 | Mine; the observatory is the user's |
| Research, an air balloon and flight | About 40 | Mine |
| Space flight | About 80 | The user's |

It is the same survivor throughout: they simply never age. *(The user's words.)* The pacing up to the keep is right and doesn't change. *(The user.)* Only the castle is designed here. The others are later projects, and a town with no people in it is an open question for later.

Rules for the whole run, proposed by me and approved by the user:

- The list stays hidden, and finishing one goal reveals the next.
- Each goal is a new kind of thing to do, not just more stones.
- It stays cosy, with no people, pressure or timers. A player can stop at any point with a home that feels complete.
- The goals are hand-made, one project at a time, not generated.

## Intent

Give someone who has finished the keep a reason to keep coming back for years: a castle grown around the keep on the hills, built in sections that each have their own finish. At the end they can move in. The camp stays below as a working farm that they can see from the walls.

The user approved this outline without changes.

## Goals

- The moment of noticing: the player climbs the keep, and one day sees something they hadn't seen before.
- The castle years feel different from laying the keep's stones. The road, cart, forge work, timber and moat each bring a different kind of work, and the later sections ask for what the camp makes, so the farm stays worth visiting. *(The user chose camp goods over a stone-heavy castle or a shorter one.)*
- A finish every season or two, so about 5 in-game years never feels like one long wait.
- The hills look more and more like a castle throughout, so even a short visit shows progress.
- Moving in feels like the end of the game, right up to the moment the next goal appears.

## Non-goals

- The next goal. This project ends with the castle finished and nothing revealed after it. The hook for the next goal comes with the town's project.
- Rooms to walk around in. Going in stays a short fade, as with the keep. *(My call, following the keep.)*
- Defence, sieges or attackers. The moat and portcullis are there to be built and looked at.
- Other people, trading, or anything new to gather or farm. Camp goods are made from what the game already produces.
- Changing the pacing or rules of anything up to the keep's finish.

## Anti-goals

- **A chore.** Nothing needs fuel, upkeep or visits. A player can leave a section half-built for years and lose nothing.
- **A loss.** Laid stone stays laid, and dug moat stays dug.
- **A required move.** Moving in is the player's choice, and the camp keeps working either way.
- **A grind of one action.** If most of those years are the same button, the design has failed. This matters more than the exact length.

## The player's path

1. The keep is finished. On a later climb, the picture from the top shows a faint line of old stones tracing where a wall could run. Nothing announces it. *(Approved by the user.)*
2. From then on the same line shows on the ground around the keep, and tapping it says what it might be. *(My call: this way a player who rarely climbs can still find it. It isn't an announcement, because it only shows on the hills.)*
3. They build a road up from the quarry. With it, the shod pony can pull a cart of stone.
4. They put up the timber frames and a hoist, and start the curtain wall.
5. They build the four corner towers, roofed with oak beams and fitted with iron.
6. They make a portcullis at the forge and build the gatehouse.
7. They dig the moat by hand. It fills with rain over the following spring.
8. They build the great hall and furnish it with beams, iron fittings and wool hangings from the loom.
9. With every other section done, they lay a feast on the hall's table from the camp's kitchen, and the castle is finished.
10. They can move in. The hall becomes home, and the camp carries on below.

The middle sections can be done in any order, unless **Building it** says otherwise. *(My call: it keeps some choice in those years.)*

## How the line of stones is revealed

- It first appears on the first climb on any day after the first climb. *(My call. The first climb stays about the view the player has earned, and the discovery comes when they go back up.)*
- It is painted into the existing view from the keep, and it looks the same in every season apart from snow.
- The climb gets no new message, and the view keeps its one season line. A journal line records seeing it, using the same approach as the hills' lines, so it isn't a blank before then.
- It works whatever season it is.

## Where it stands and how it looks

- On the hills, around the keep at (18, 10). The keep remains the castle's central tower.
- The whole castle must fit the hills map and must not cover the outcrop at (10, 7), the hills exit or the path. A pony spot it would cover may move, because by then the pony has usually been won over. If it hasn't, it still needs somewhere to graze. The exact footprint is left to the builder.
- The survivor walks around the outside and through the gatehouse into the yard. The walls block sight correctly, as tall sprites do. *(The game has no collisions today. Whether walls stop the survivor is left to the builder, but nothing may trap them.)*
- Everything is drawn in code from the existing stone palette, with no image files. Winter puts snow on wall tops, tower tops and roofs. The moat freezes in winter.
- Once the castle is finished, the hall's windows are lit at night whether or not the player is there.
- The view from the keep shows each finished section, so climbing stays worthwhile.

## Building it

*(All numbers are my call. The sections and their kinds of work were approved by the user.)*

The target is about 200 in-game days of steady play, or roughly 5 years. A player with the mine and iron pickaxe can dig up to 4 stone a day, plus a little rubble, so about 3 a day is a steady rate. The castle uses about 300 stone, so stone is roughly half the time and the rest is other work.

| Section | Needs | Its own finish | Rough days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Road, quarry to keep | 30 stone laid along the way, in stages | The road is drawn in, and the pony can pull the cart | 15 |
| Cart | Workshop item: wood and ore | The cart stands at the quarry | A few |
| Timber frames and hoist | Wood from the stores, at the wall | Scaffolding rises, and high walls can start | 10 |
| Curtain wall | 120 stone, one side at a time | Each side is drawn complete | 45 |
| Corner towers | 20 stone each, four towers, with oak beams and iron fittings for each roof | Each tower has its own roof | 30 |
| Portcullis and gatehouse | Forge: ore and wood. Then 30 stone | The portcullis drops, then rises, on finishing | 20 |
| Moat | Dug by hand, a stretch per go, with a daily limit | Fills over the following spring | 20, then the wait |
| Great hall | 40 stone, oak beams, iron fittings and wool hangings | Roof on, walls hung | 40 |
| The feast | Dishes from the camp's kitchen, set on the hall's table | The castle is finished | 15 |

**Camp goods** *(the idea was approved by the user; the items and amounts are my call)*:

- **Oak beams:** a new Workshop item made from stored wood, several at a time.
- **Iron fittings:** a new Forge item made from ore and a little wood. The outcrop's daily limit paces them.
- **Wool hangings:** a new Loom item made from wool. Wool comes from the once-a-year shearing, so asking for more than one shearing's worth makes the hall span more than a year on purpose.
- **The feast:** the table asks for a set of dishes the game can already make, such as bread, apple pie, honey cake and stew. Each dish is carried up and set down once. Dishes on the table never spoil.
- Goods are carried up in the bag, or drawn from the stores where the camp's stores already reach, and every item is one action at the site. None is lost, and all of them can wait any length of time.

**The cart** *(the user chose this behaviour; the details are my call)*:

- It needs the road and the shod pony, and it holds 40 stone, in a store of its own that takes no bag space.
- While the cart stands at the quarry, stone dug there goes straight into it until it's full, then into the bag as usual. The player can also take a load from it into the bag.
- With the shod pony along, the cart follows the player between the quarry and the hills. At the site, laying draws from the cart first, then the bag. The stone can't be used anywhere else.
- The pony still won't go up in snow, so the cart waits at the quarry in winter and keeps filling, which makes a big first haul in spring. In winter, stone goes up by bag. A cart caught on the hills when winter comes stays there until spring, and laying from it still works.
- The quarry's daily limit doesn't change.

- **No saving up:** every stone counts as soon as it's laid, as with the keep.
- **Timber** can come from the camp's stores, as the keep's door wood does.
- **Order:** the road and cart come first, and the frames come before the wall. The wall comes before the towers and the gatehouse. The moat can be dug at any point after the wall, and the hall can be started at any point after the frames. Otherwise the player chooses.
- **What finishes it:** the feast can only be laid once every other section is done. A moat that is fully dug counts as done, even if it's still waiting for rain. *(My call. It keeps the finish and moving in as one moment.)*
- **The moat fills:** a finished moat fills over the next spring's showers, a little each rainy day, and is full by summer. Nothing is required of the player. If the game's spring has no rain on some days, it just takes those days off.
- **Any season:** everything except the cart works year-round, and digging the moat stops while the ground is frozen in winter.
- **Tired survivors** get the usual refusal.
- **The finish:** when the last dish goes on the table, the message says plainly that the castle is finished. There is a journal line and a morning note. Each section also gets a morning note, as the keep's first stone does.

## Moving in

- Once the castle is finished, the hall's door is a tap target, as the keep's is. Going in at night sleeps there, as in the keep today.
- The first time they go in, the player is offered the choice to make the hall their home. They can say no, and they can change their mind at either door later. *(My call. The user said the player "can" move in, and I've taken that to mean it's not forced.)*
- **What home means:** resting, cooking and the warm rest for a sick day work at the hall when it is home, with a fire that needs no wood, as the keep's does. *(My call, following the keep's fire.)* The stores stay at camp. Materials still settle at camp and can be spent from anywhere that allows it today.
- **The camp keeps working:** beds, field, sheep, bakery, chickens, orchard and hive all stay at camp and carry on as they do when the player is out. Nothing there gets worse for being lived away from. The house stays standing.
- **Friends and the pony:** the shod pony lives at the castle when the hall is home, apart from winter, when it waits at the foot of the path as it does today. Other friends stay at camp, as with a night in the keep. Check how their gifts and the bird's alarm behave, as the keep's design warned.
- **The view from the walls:** from the top, the view stays the painted one, and the camp stays visible in it.

## Journal and notes

- New journal lines: seeing the line of stones, the road finished, each major section, the moat filling, the feast, and the castle finished. They stay off the pages until they happen.
- Copy is plain and warm, not poetic, with no exclamation marks.

## Secrecy

The castle stays out of the README and other player-facing text until the player finds it, as an `EASTER EGG:` feature under `AGENTS.md`, because "the list stays hidden" is the point. Add it to the list of current easter eggs in `AGENTS.md`. Tests cover it like anything else. *(My call, following the approved rule. It changes how the keep was treated, since the keep is in the README, and that stays as it is.)*

## Save format

This changes the saved shape, so it takes the next `SAVE_VERSION` with an upgrade step, and it must be the only open PR doing so. Existing saves begin with nothing revealed and nothing built. A save whose keep is already finished sees the line on its next climb, as long as it's a later day than the first climb. If saves don't record a first climb, treat the next climb as the first.

Validation should reject impossible states, including stone counts out of range, a section built before what it depends on, and a home in a castle that isn't finished, and a cart that holds more than 40 stone.

## Constraints

- TypeScript, Phaser 3, Vite, Vitest and oxlint, with no new dependencies and no image files.
- Follow the existing patterns: `applyAction` handlers, `pickTapTarget`, `prompts.ts`, `newsBetween`, `JOURNAL_PAGES`, `WORKSHOP_UPGRADES`, `src/ui/panels.ts`, and `src/scene/keepViewArt.ts` for the picture.
- SonarCloud needs 80% coverage on new code and `App.ts` is untested, so keep logic in tested helpers.
- Everything is reachable by tap as well as by keyboard, and every path must still work when the survivor is weak.
- Extend the scripted player in `tests/playthrough/` to reach the finished castle and move in. Use it to check that the total comes out at about 200 days of steady play, and tune the numbers if it doesn't. Keep that test fast enough for every run.
- Verify with `npm test`, `npm run lint` and `npm run build`, plus a browser check at desktop and phone sizes of the real path: seeing the line, building a part of each section by key and by tap, the moat filling, moving in, moving back, and saving and reloading at each stage.
- This is a large project. It should be built in stages that each ship on their own, with each stage leaving the game whole.

## Left to the builder

- The castle's exact footprint, how the walls are drawn, and how many pictures each section has, as long as progress shows every few stones.
- Small numbers, as long as the castle stays about 200 days of steady play and has a finish every season or two.
- The wording of lines, within the copy rules.
- How the work is split into stages.
