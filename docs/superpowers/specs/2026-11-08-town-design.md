# The Town Design

## The long view

This is the next goal after the castle in the run of hidden goals described in the [castle design](2026-11-07-castle-design.md): keep, castle, town, palace with an observatory, research and flight, then space flight. Each goal feels like the final one and really does get finished, and then there is one more.

Two things change for the town:

- **About 20 in-game years.** That's about 800 days, roughly four times the castle. *(The user's figure. It replaces the castle spec's "about 10".)*
- **People.** The game has been the survivor alone until now, and everything before the town stays that way. A town needs people, *"otherwise we are just creating a zombie apocalypse"*. *(The user's words. They replace the earlier rule of "no people".)*

The other rules stand. The list stays hidden, and finishing one goal reveals the next. Each goal is a new kind of thing to do. It stays cosy, with no pressure or timers. The goals are hand-made.

**Why the long goals exist.** *"We dont really expect anyone to do this. We are currently looking at long term goals to allow longer play."* *(The user.)* Most players will never finish the town. It's there so that someone who wants to keep going can. So each step must be pleasant on its own, and the game must feel complete at any point a player stops. The 20 years is a ceiling, not a number to tune toward.

## Intent

After the castle, people start to come. The first is a lost traveller who needs a roof for the night, just as the survivor once did. They stay if there's work. From then on, each house the survivor builds brings one more person, who takes over one of the survivor's regular jobs. Over the years a feudal castle town grows on the hills around the castle, and the survivor's days change from doing chores to building it. The town is finished when every house is full and the market square opens. Market day brings traders from beyond the hills, and the first of them quietly reveals the next goal.

## Goals

- The first person is a big, quiet moment. It's the first other person in the whole game, and it echoes how the game began.
- The town feels lived in: people go about their work and say hello as you pass.
- Each house is a small finish, and there's a finish about every season or two (10 to 20 days at most). The castle's lesson was that a finish should never be far off.
- The survivor's days change over the years. Jobs are handed over one by one, and the freed time goes into building.
- At any point, the town so far feels finished. Three houses and three neighbours is a complete little town.
- The market gives the camp's surplus somewhere to go, and brings in what the hills can't make. That sets up the next goal.

## Non-goals

- Working out the palace, or anything after it. This project ends with the town finished and one hint of what's next, as described under **The finish and the hook**.
- Rooms inside houses. Going in is not a thing. Houses are seen from outside.
- Money, prices, shops or a trading screen.
- New crops or gatherable foods. *(A standing rule: the game is not a farming sim.)*
- Changing anything before the castle is finished, except where workers take over existing jobs.

## Anti-goals

- **Animal Crossing.** *(The user's words.)* So: no conversations to keep up, no friendship levels, no gifts or favourite items, no birthdays, no requests, errands or quests, and no moods or complaints. Townsfolk are part of the place, not a second game.
- **Renaming people.** *"people are people and already have names."* *(The user.)* Townsfolk arrive with their own names, and the player never renames them. Animals are still named as they are now.
- **A chore or a loss.** Nothing needs upkeep. No one leaves because of neglect, nothing a worker does can fail, and anything built stays built.
- **A grind of one action.** If most of the 20 years is the same button, the design has failed.

## The player's path

1. **The traveller arrives.** On an evening after the survivor has made the hall their home, a lost traveller turns up at the hall door, unannounced, needing shelter. Nothing before this hints at a town. *(My guess, which the user accepted.)* Moving in is what opens the town. A player who keeps the house as home never sees the traveller. *(The user chose this when asked.)* Moving in stays optional for the castle itself.
2. **They ask for work.** The traveller sleeps in the hall. In the morning, they ask if there's work. If the survivor says yes, they stay. If the survivor says no, they stay another night and ask again. After three refusals they move on. *(The user.)* Whether they, or someone else, can come back later is left to the builder, as long as the town can't be lost for good.
3. **They take a job.** A worker takes over one of the survivor's regular jobs. *(The user confirmed this.)* The traveller needs somewhere to live, so the first house is built for them. Where they sleep until it's built is left to the builder.
4. **Houses bring people.** *"word gets around, you build them houses."* *(The user.)* The survivor builds a house on a plot outside the castle walls. Some time after it's finished, a new person arrives and asks for work. Saying yes gives them the house and a job. One house means one person and one job.
5. **The town grows.** Houses fill the open ground on the hills. Partway through, new ground opens for more houses, as described under **Where it stands**.
6. **The square takes shape alongside.** The square is built up in pieces over the years: its paving, a well, and the stalls one at a time. It isn't saved for the end.
7. **The finish.** *"every house is full and the market square opens."* *(The user.)* Market day comes, and the town is finished.

## The people

- **Who they are.** Ordinary people, each with their own name. They arrive one at a time, for their own reasons, and each says one plain line about where they've come from.
- **Walking through the town.** *"people going about their jobs, saying hello as you pass."* *(The user.)* A greeting is their name and a short line, perhaps varying with the season or the weather. That's all.
- **Where you see them.** Their jobs are the survivor's old jobs, so they're seen working across the map: at the pen, the coop, the beds, the woodpile, the orchard. In the evening they walk home to the hills. The camp below gets busier too. *(My guess, which the user accepted.)*
- **What they need.** Nothing. They feed and house themselves. They don't need to be fed, kept warm or visited, and they can't fall sick or leave.

## Jobs

- **What a job is.** A worker takes over one regular job that the survivor does now. Examples are collecting eggs and honey, taking loaves from the bakery, keeping the wood store stocked, sowing and harvesting the beds, working the field and the mill, feeding and shearing the sheep, and picking the orchard. The exact list, and how jobs are grouped into one worker each, is left to the builder. Each job goes to one person.
- **More houses than camp jobs.** The camp has roughly 8 to 10 jobs, but a 20-year town has more houses than that. Once every camp job is taken, later newcomers keep the square instead: the well, the paving and, above all, the stalls. So the last people to arrive are the market's stallholders, and the square can't open until each stall has someone. *(My call.)*
- **Which job a newcomer takes.** Who chooses is left to the builder. A newcomer could ask for a particular job, or the survivor could offer one. Either way it's a single plain choice, with no menu of stats.
- **Reliable and free.** Workers do their job every day without fail. There are no wages, moods or failures. *(My guess, which the user accepted.)*
- **Output.** A worker's output goes where the survivor's would have gone: the stores, the larder, the castle goods. Whatever won't fit is simply left for another day, with nothing wasted and no warning. *(My call.)* Because they never miss a day, a job with a worker produces more over time than most players manage. That surplus is what fills the market later.
- **The survivor can still do a job by hand.** A job handed over is never taken away from the player. Whether doing it alongside a worker adds anything is left to the builder.

## Where it stands

- **Around the castle on the hills, as a feudal castle town.** *(The user.)* Houses gather outside the walls with the castle standing above them. The road from the quarry and the path up from camp become its streets.
- **Fixed, designed plots.** Houses go on plots, as the camp's buildings do. They are never placed freely. *(My guess, which the user accepted.)*
- **Room.** The hills are 40 by 30 tiles. The castle and moat take the middle-left, and the ore outcrop sits to the west. The open ground to the south and east fits perhaps 8 to 12 houses with lanes between them. That's the first part of the town.
- **New ground partway through.** A 20-year town needs more room than that. Partway through, new ground opens next to the hills for more houses, with something to build to reach it, such as a bridge. It's a mid-town surprise, as the moat filling was for the castle. What it is, and when it opens, is left to the builder.
- **The square.** It goes somewhere central among the houses. It's built up in pieces, and it's where market day happens.

## Building it

- **Houses in stages.** Each house goes up in steps, such as footings, walls, roof, door and hearth, each a small finish, as the hall was. The last step makes it ready, and word starts to get around.
- **Materials.** Houses are built from what the camp and quarry already make: stone, beams, iron fittings, wool hangings and wood. There's no new resource to gather. *(My guess, which the user accepted. It follows the castle's approved rule of asking for camp goods rather than piling on stone.)*
- **The pace comes from goods and arrivals, not stone counts.** Word takes a while to get around after each house, so a player can leave and come back. Early workers' output helps pay for later houses, so the town speeds up a little as it fills.
- **Variety.** Over 20 years, the work should keep changing: houses, the square's paving, a well, the stalls, and the way to the new ground. Anything else is left to the builder, as long as it fits a small feudal town and stays clear of farming.
- **Any order, mostly.** Within what's open, the player picks which house or square piece to work on next.

## Market day and the finish

- **The finish.** When the last house is full, the square opens, and the first market day is the town's finish.
- **What's sold.** The surplus from workers' jobs goes to the stalls instead of piling up in stores. On market day the square fills with what the town makes: bread, honey, wool, apples and eggs. *(The user approved this.)*
- **Traders.** Market day draws traders from beyond the hills. They take the town's surplus and bring things the survivor has never had, such as glass, brass and spices. There's no money, no prices and no trading screen. Market day happens, and things arrive. *(The user approved this.)*
- **How often.** How often market day comes after the first, and whether its goods have a use before the next goal, is left to the builder. It should never be needed for anything already in the game.

## The finish and the hook

On the first market day, a trader's cart carries something no one in the town could make, such as a glass lens. That's the quiet first hint of the palace and its observatory. The survivor keeps the lens, and it gets a journal line, but it has no use yet. *(My call.)* The town project ends there. The palace isn't designed here, and nothing else about it shows. *(My proposal, which the user approved.)*

## Journal and notes

- **Journal lines** for the traveller arriving, the first person staying, each new neighbour (with their name), the square opening and the first market day. They stay off the pages until they happen.
- **Morning notes** for a newcomer at the door, and for a house finished.
- **Copy** is plain and warm, not poetic, with no exclamation marks, as before.

## Secrecy

The town is an `EASTER EGG:` feature under `AGENTS.md`, like the castle. It stays out of the README and other player-facing text, and it's added to the list of current easter eggs there.

## Save format

This adds people, houses and the square to the saved state, so it needs the next `SAVE_VERSION` with an upgrade step. It must be the only open PR doing so. Existing saves start with no town. A save whose hall is already home sees the traveller on a later evening.

Validation should reject impossible states. Examples are a person with no house who isn't the traveller, two people in one house, two people on one job, a job handed over that doesn't exist yet, a house's stages out of order, the square open before every house is full, and any town before the castle is finished.

## Constraints

- TypeScript, Phaser, Vite, Vitest and oxlint. No new dependencies and no image files. Sprites are drawn in code as the others are.
- Follow the existing patterns: `applyAction` handlers, `pickTapTarget`, `prompts.ts`, `newsBetween`, `JOURNAL_PAGES`, `src/ui/panels.ts` with tested views, and the castle's site rules in `src/game/castleSites.ts`.
- SonarCloud needs 80% coverage on new code, and `App.ts` and the scene classes are untested. Keep logic in tested helpers.
- Everything is reachable by tap as well as by key, and works when the survivor is weak.
- The scripted player in `tests/playthrough/` plays the start of the town: the traveller, the first few houses and their workers. Not all 800 days. It checks that no finish is far from the last one. Keep that test fast.
- Verify with `npm test`, `npm run lint`, `npm run build`, and a browser check at desktop and phone sizes of whatever each stage adds.
- Build it in slices that each ship on their own and leave the game whole. The first slice is the traveller, the first house and the first worker. Later slices add more houses and jobs, the square, the new ground, and market day with the hook.

## Left to the builder

- The plots, the lanes, the square's place and the new ground, within the rules above.
- How many houses, the stages of a house, and the exact numbers, as long as there's a finish every season or two.
- The list of jobs and how a newcomer's job is chosen.
- How long word takes to get around, and how often market day comes.
- How people are drawn and how they move, as long as they're seen at their work and walk home at evening.
- Names, greetings and the wording of lines, within the copy rules.
- How the work is split into slices after the first.
