# Playthrough check: a new camp to the finished keep

31 scripted games played through the real rules, using the same walking, clock and journal as the game. No game was changed for this check. Balance and wording changes are left for a decision.

## In short

- **No dead ends.** All 31 games reached the finished keep, slept in it and climbed it. Nothing the panels offered was ever refused once it was paid for.
- **About two to three in-game years.** The middle game took 78 days (1.9 years) played actively and 120 days (3 years) played slowly.
- **No long empty stretches.** The longest gap between milestones was 9 to 15 days in a middle game, and 25 at worst.
- **The late game has the guidance gaps.** Nothing says the keep has opened. The journal's "what next" line stops helping well before the end.

## How it was played

| Style | What it does | Games |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Orderly | Takes offered work in panel order | 1 (repeated seeds played almost identically) |
| Wandering | Picks offered work at random | 15 |
| Unhurried | Wanders, and dawdles 15 seconds between jobs | 15 |

The player only starts work the panels show, plus exploring the paths, the sites and the animals. It looks after itself, keeps a fire, and lays food by for winter.

### What to keep in mind

- **It can't judge feel.** It can't say whether any of this is fun or charming.
- **Play hours are a floor.** It never stops to read, look around or decide, and a key press counts as one second. A real player will take longer, possibly much longer.
- **It knows the rules.** It was written from the code, so it never loses time working out what something means. Guidance gaps come from reading what the game says at each step, not from the bot getting lost.
- **Early deaths were the bot's fault.** Early versions died by letting the fire go out with fish in the bag, or by reaching winter with no fishing line. I fixed those, so the clean sickness record below shows a careful player, not a careless one.

## Dead ends

None found. The test in `tests/playthrough.test.ts` now guards this. I broke the game on purpose in two ways, a door that can't be hung and a forge that can't be afforded, and it failed both times.

The wolf house, fox den and birdhouse were sometimes left unbuilt. Each waits on a wild animal choosing to stay, which depends on chance and on the player leaving food out, so this isn't a dead end.

## Guidance gaps

**1. Nothing says the keep has opened (the largest gap).** The keep's site appears on the hills once the forge stands, but the player is told nothing. The forge's message is about the workshop and ore. The only mention is the hinges row: "For the door of the keep, when its walls are ready." A player who has no reason to walk back up to the hills could miss the game's final goal. The bot only found it because it was written to look.

**2. The journal's "what next" line stops at the homestead.** It never mentions the hills, the forge, the pony or the keep. In the middle game it read "You have followed every path. Your camp is home, and the woods are yours to wander." for the last 47 days (wandering) or 65 days (unhurried) before the keep was done. The journal's list of regions does show the unexplored area past the quarry, which helps a little. From the code, that line could also appear before the hills are found, but this didn't happen in any of these games.

**3. "No structures left to build" isn't always true.** The build list said this for 25 to 57 days in the middle game while something could still be built. The loom is the clearest case, because it stays hidden until the first shearing. The animal houses are the others.

Everything else the bot took from code knowledge has guidance in the game: the panel rows, prompts near each place, or morning notes. That covers ore, the pony's food, the pips, the sheep's feed and the keep's door.

## Pacing

**Early game: busy and safe.** The shelter comes on day 1 and the cold night passes on day 4. The first winter (days 11–20) has the least food, and going into it without a fishing line starved one early version of the bot by day 19. With the line made, no game fell sick.

**One missed spring costs a whole year.** Grain can only be sown in spring, and the steps after it all depend on that:

| Step | Field ready for the first spring (orderly) | Spring missed (wandering, middle) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First grain threshed | Day 36 | Day 75 |
| Sheep arrive (they need straw) | Day 37 | Day 77 |
| First shearing (spring only) | Day 61 | Day 101 |
| Loom | Day 61 | Day 101 |

So the loom often comes after the keep is finished, which was day 78 in the same middle game. Nothing is broken here, but the order may not be what you'd expect.

**The keep comes quickly once started.** The first stone to the last took about 20 days when played actively and 28 when played slowly. The iron pickaxe and the mine do a lot of that. It is still the game's longest late stretch, and it is one job repeated.

**The bag fills often.** The bot ran out of room 38 to 91 times in a middle game, roughly every day or two. It doesn't plan its bag the way a person might, so treat this as a rough figure. Food can't be set down, only eaten or put in the larder, so before the larder a bag full of food has to be eaten down.

## Decisions for you

1. **Say the keep has opened?** I'd add one line when the forge goes up. This is the one gap that could hide the ending.
2. **Extend the journal's "what next" line?** I'd add hills, forge and keep steps. It's a small change, and it would also fix the early "every path" line.
3. **Reword "No structures left to build"?** I'd make it something like "Nothing to build for now", which stays true.
4. **Is the pace right?** Two to three years to the keep, with the keep itself taking 20 to 28 days. That's a question of feel and I can't answer it. It's also worth a real playthrough before growing the keep into a castle.
5. **Is a year's wait after a missed spring OK?** I'd leave it. It suits a game about the seasons, and nothing is lost by it.
